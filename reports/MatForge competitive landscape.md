# MatForge wins by verifying, not generating

Photo-to-material generation stopped being a differentiator in August 2025, when Chaos shipped its own AI Material Generator inside V-Ray 7.2. Corona 14 followed in November 2025, and both reached 4K by May 2026. It comes bundled with the subscriptions every MatForge customer already pays for, so MatForge cannot win on "photo in, material out". What nobody ships, as far as these notes could find, is the other half of the product. Chaos's generator outputs only albedo, normal and roughness. The one third-party AI rival, MatSnap AI, is Corona-only, hallucinates its albedo with Gemini, and derives every other map heuristically. No vendor or script sells a rules-based, scene-wide material auditor that fixes what it finds. Nobody fits materials against the user's own renderer either, or ties output to measured physics such as metal n,k, glass absorption and calibrated albedo. MatForge's defensible position is to be the layer that proves a material is physically correct in V-Ray and in Corona, and fixes it when it is not. That includes materials made by Chaos's own generator. This changes the v5 plan in three concrete ways. The AI module becomes a "fit and verify" engine rather than a generator. OpenPBR 1.1.1 becomes the literal canonical format instead of "OpenPBR-style". And the Inspector must be proven in a real 3ds Max session before anything else, because today its 58 tests have only run off-Max. "Ten years ahead" is not something any product can honestly claim against research labs. The achievable version is narrower and real: ship now, inside Max, capabilities that exist elsewhere only as research papers or closed measurement ecosystems, and build the calibration data that a fast follower cannot copy in one release cycle.

## Chaos shipped photo-to-material in nine months, and stopped at three maps

The renderer vendor is the competitor that matters. **V-Ray 7 Update 2 for 3ds Max (August 2025) introduced the AI Material Generator.** It takes a reference photo uploaded to Chaos Cloud and returns a PBR material with options for resolution, tiling, de-lighting and normal/roughness intensity. It launched as an experimental beta included with all V-Ray subscriptions, and Chaos gave no answer on post-beta pricing or quotas ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/08/chaos-adds-new-ai-tools-to-v-ray-in-v-ray-7-for-3ds-max-update-2/)). **Corona 14 (12 November 2025) added the same generator inside Chaos Cosmos.** It outputs **albedo, normal and roughness**, and organisations with AI-use policies can disable it ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/11/chaos-releases-corona-14-for-3ds-max-and-cinema-4d/), [Chaos press](https://www.chaos.com/press/corona-14-faster-more-expressive-storytelling-with-ai)). **V-Ray 7.3 (23 April 2026) and Corona 15 (27 May 2026) both raised the ceiling to 4K** ([CG Channel V-Ray 7.3](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/04/chaos-releases-v-ray-7-update-3-for-3ds-max/), [CG Channel Corona 15](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/05/chaos-releases-corona-15-for-3ds-max-and-blender/)). Corona 15 also bundles Veras generative ideation for free and adds a Glints layer to CoronaPhysicalMtl. Corona Solo costs **$414/yr** ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2026/05/chaos-releases-corona-15-for-3ds-max-and-blender/)). Around the generator sits **Chaos Scans**, which Chaos's own page counts as "over 2,500" in one place and "3,000+" in another. It describes them as generated from thousands of real-world images, not as BRDF-measured ([chaos.com/scans](https://www.chaos.com/scans)). Cosmos also gained roughly **30,000 assets at no extra cost** in July 2025 ([Chaos press](https://www.chaos.com/press/chaos-adds-30000-assets-to-chaos-cosmos-making-it-the-largest-smartest-curated-3d-asset-library-for-aec-professionals)). These notes found no V-Ray 8 as of November 2026, and no evidence that Chaos audits or validates existing scene materials.

Two inferences follow. First, Chaos moved from beta to 4K in about nine months across two products. Any MatForge feature built only on a commodity model should be assumed copyable within one or two Chaos release cycles. Second, the stated output list has no metalness, IOR, glass absorption, SSS or height, and no physical scale. That gap is exactly where MatForge's planned physics lives. (Whether V-Ray 7.3 added maps is unverified.)

The third-party field is fragmented into single-purpose tools, and none of them combines generation with validation:

### MatSnap shows what an AI-first competitor gets wrong

MatSnap AI is the only direct third-party analogue to MatForge's generator. It was announced on 10 April 2026 as a one-time Gumroad/ScriptSpot purchase that uses a bring-your-own Google Gemini key ([CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/matsnap-ai-material-generator-for-3ds-max.html)). The vendor demo shows the flow ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4XxblUY0Lo)). The user loads or pastes any image, even a video still, and right-clicks a patch. Gemini vision names the patch (for example "Deep Forest Green Polished Marble"). The AI ("Nano Banana 2") then generates **only the diffuse map**, at 512 to 4K. Normal, bump, gloss and displacement are derived locally from that diffuse. Seams are fixed by hand in a 2×2 offset/blend editor. Hue, saturation, gamma, roughness, bump and displacement are global sliders, and in the demo the bump came out inverted and needed an "Invert bump" checkbox. The output is a full Slate network for Corona Physical or Legacy materials, saved as a .mat file. There is no V-Ray support, no metalness, IOR or physical scale, one material at a time, and no scene audit. This matters strategically because MatSnap is the "AI-first, physics-never" design taken to its end point. The albedo is invented by an image model, not measured, and every downstream map inherits that guess. MatSnap shows customer appetite for a dockable in-Max panel, and it also shows the class of error the Inspector should catch.

## Research has solved map generation and left roughness unsolved

The academic state of the art makes five-map generation a commodity, with two caveats MatForge can exploit: licences and roughness. **Ubisoft's CHORD (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025)** is the strongest openly benchmarked single-image estimator. It runs a two-stage chained diffusion and estimates SVBRDF channels one at a time in **2.1 s on an RTX A6000**, about 11× faster than RGB-X ([arXiv 2509.09952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09952)). Its weights carry a **"Research-Only - Copyleft" licence behind a gated repo** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-chord)), so it cannot ship in a commercial plugin. Its numbers are still the best available yardstick. On a 250-material Substance test set it reports basecolor PSNR 29.05 against RGB-X 25.72 and MatFusion 25.35. **Roughness is the weakest channel for every method, at PSNR 14–17.5** ([CHORD paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09952v1)). The authors self-report these figures on synthetic crops, and no independent replication was found.

The commercially usable routes are narrow. **fal PATINA** returns basecolor, normal, roughness, metalness and height up to 8K for **$0.08 per 1K five-map set**, under a "Commercial use" label ([fal](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/patina/material)). **Marigold-IID** outputs albedo, roughness and metallicity under CreativeML Open RAIL++-M, which allows commercial use with use-based restrictions ([HF](https://huggingface.co/prs-eth/marigold-iid-appearance-v1-1)). **NVIDIA's Cosmos-based DiffusionRenderer** is a 7B, video-oriented model under the NVIDIA Open Model License, which permits commercial use. It outputs G-buffers rather than tileable maps ([HF](https://huggingface.co/zianw/Diffusion_Renderer_Inverse_Cosmos_7B)). Adobe's Image to Material, shipped since Sampler 4.2 in 2023, exists only inside the Sampler app ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/adobe-releases-substance-3d-sampler-4-2)). Adobe Research's **MaterialPicker (SIGGRAPH 2025)** treats material maps as video frames and handles angled or occluded photo crops ([Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/materialpicker-multi-modal-dit-based-material-generation)). Its code and weight status is unconfirmed. The **MatSynth** dataset of 4,000+ tileable 4K materials is mostly CC0 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gvecchio/MatSynth)), so it offers a licence-clean training and benchmark base.

Procedural generation has moved to vision-language program synthesis. Adobe's **MultiMat (ICLR 2026)** generates Substance graphs with constrained tree search and per-node validation, and reports KID 2.365 against VLMaterial's 14.155 ([arXiv 2509.22151](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22151v2)). The output is resolution-independent and editable, but it needs a graph evaluator and baking before VRayMtl or Corona can use it. Capture research is older and has been absorbed into the models. Flash/no-flash phone SVBRDF capture dates back to 2015 ([Aittala et al.](https://reality.tf.fau.de/projects/two-shot-svbrdf/two-shot-svbrdf.pdf)), and MatFusion now conditions directly on flash or flash/no-flash pairs ([arXiv 2406.06539](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.06539)). No commercially licensed, downloadable flash-conditioned model was found.

What this means for MatForge is an inference, but a firm one. Roughness and metalness are the channels every model gets worst, and they are also the channels that most decide whether a render looks expensive or fake. A product that constrains those two channels with physics, and checks them by rendering, attacks the field's measured weak point rather than competing on its strength. The current code also needs a correction. `forge_fal.py` calls **`fal-ai/patina/material`**, passing the photo as `image_url` alongside a prompt. The notes identify that endpoint as **text-to-PBR with optional img2img**, while **`fal-ai/patina/material/extract`** is the one that pulls a material from a photo ([fal docs](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/patina/material), [fal blog](https://blog.fal.ai/introducing-patina)). So MatForge's "Ultra" engine may be reinterpreting the reference instead of extracting it. That is the same hallucination problem MatSnap has, and it must be tested before any accuracy claim goes out.

## OpenPBR became the default while Corona stayed silent

The second shift the v5 plan did not capture is standards. **OpenPBR Surface reached v1.1.1 on 17 April 2026.** That release formally lets implementers use approximations and revises the thin-film section ([OpenPBR releases](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenPBR/releases)). **3ds Max 2026 made OpenPBR its default material**, upgraded MaterialX to 1.38.10-OpenPBR, and exports OpenPBR and MaterialX through USD ([CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/autodesk-releases-3ds-max-2026.html)). **V-Ray 7.2 added an OpenPBR shading mode** to the V-Ray Material ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/08/chaos-adds-new-ai-tools-to-v-ray-in-v-ray-7-for-3ds-max-update-2/)). Redshift made OpenPBR the default for new materials in Cinema 4D 2026.2 ([Maxon](https://help.maxon.net/c4d/2027/en-us/Content/html/COM_REDSHIFT3D_REDSHIFT4C4D_NODES_CORE_OPENPBRMATERIAL.html)), and Omniverse Kit 110 added an OpenPBR template in March 2026 ([NVIDIA](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/materials-and-rendering/latest/materials_release-notes.html)). **Corona 15 coverage mentions no OpenPBR, MaterialX or spectral support** ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=176209)). Corona OpenPBR therefore stays unverified, and it is the clearest translation gap in the market. At the measurement-grade end, **X-Rite PANTORA 2026.1 (March 2026)** distributes AxF files to VRED, Iray and the Virtual Light Booth, with **no V-Ray or Corona plug-in listed** ([X-Rite](https://www.xrite.com/fr-fr/service-support/downloads/p/pantora-2026_1)).

The implication is structural. The spec now allows approximation, and engines adopt it unevenly. The same OpenPBR material will render differently in Max's native shader, in V-Ray's OpenPBR mode, and in Corona, which has no native support. A tool that measures and corrects those differences has a durable job, because adopting the standard creates the problem rather than solving it. This also turns the Inspector's scope from an assumption into a requirement. Every new Max 2026+ scene defaults to OpenPBR materials, so an Inspector that audits only VRayMtl and CoronaPhysicalMtl will miss the default material in the host application.

## The next decade rewards measurement, render-in-the-loop fitting and agents

Look 5 to 10 years ahead and the frontier sorts into four trends at very different maturities. **Neural materials are shipping first in real-time.** NVIDIA's RTX Neural Materials claim up to 5× faster processing of layered offline-grade shaders, and Neural Texture Compression claims up to 7× VRAM savings ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-rtx-neural-rendering-introduces-next-era-of-ai-powered-graphics-innovation)). These notes found no offline renderer that supports them as of November 2026, so they are plausibly 3–7 years from archviz. **Inverse rendering with Gaussian splats is the most active research line of 2026.** MaterialClusterGS, AEGIR, IR-HGP (CVPR 2026) and RadioGS all recover PBR materials from multi-view capture ([AEGIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28635), [IR-HGP](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/39425), [RadioGS](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01491v1)). Corona 14 already renders splats ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2025/11/chaos-releases-corona-14-for-3ds-max-and-cinema-4d/)). These methods target objects and scenes rather than tileable surfaces, but they point to a future where one phone walk-through of a client's room yields every material in it. **Spectral and micro-detail effects are moving into RGB engines**: a 2026 paper encodes spectral behaviour for RGB pipelines ([arXiv 2602.18741](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18741)), OpenPBR standardises thin film, and Corona 15 ships glints. **Agents are arriving in DCCs, but the vendor versions are still shallow.** The Autodesk Assistant in 3ds Max 2027 is a documentation Q&A tech preview with no scene editing ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2027/ENU/3DSMax-What-s-New/files/GUID-92EF3530-9E04-46DF-B3F2-2BA150C6CEDF.htm)). A community `3dsmax-mcp` already lets Claude read and modify Max scenes ([CGPress](https://cgpress.org/archives/3dsmax-mcp-connects-claude-to-3ds-max.html)), and Blender MCP servers already produce scored material validation reports ([glama](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/Mishaadevv/blender-mcp)).

Being "10 years ahead" therefore cannot mean inventing what CVPR publishes next year. Labs with thousands of GPUs set that curve, and MatForge consumes it. It can honestly mean three things, each grounded in evidence. First, **productise what is research today**: a render-in-the-loop fit inside the user's own V-Ray or Corona. That is a practical, non-differentiable version of the Mitsuba/Dr.Jit idea, and these notes found no Max plugin doing it (absence of evidence, not proof). Second, **own data no fast follower can generate in a release cycle**: per-renderer-version roughness transfer curves, glass absorption fits, metal n,k tables, and a real-photo benchmark set with ground truth. The field has no standard benchmark on real phone photos, because CHORD and others score on synthetic crops. Third, **stand at the junction the trends converge on**: an auditable, deterministic material-correctness engine that agents call as a tool. When splat capture, neural bakes and agent-driven scene building arrive, each needs a verifier that says "this material is physically wrong, and here is the fix". The moat is that verifier, plus its calibration corpus. Generation is not the moat.

## What the v5 plan must change

The v5 spec's opening assessment of Chaos names only Chaos Scans ("3,100+ scanned materials") as the vendor threat. It ranks the AI Generator last as "hardest to make physically correct" (`docs/plan/1_product_spec.md`, sections 0–1). The module order (Inspector → Builder + Capture → AI) still holds, and is strengthened. Four assumptions need revising. **First, Module C's job changes from "generate" to "fit and certify".** Chaos gives three-map generation away free, so MatForge's AI module should accept maps from any source: Chaos's generator, PATINA, MatSnap, Megascans, or a capture. It then constrains them with physics, fits them by rendering, and stamps them with a verified ΔE. The Chaos generator becomes an upstream feeder rather than a rival. **Second, the canonical representation should be OpenPBR 1.1.1 itself**, not "OpenPBR-style parameters", with VRayMtl, CoronaPhysicalMtl and Max OpenPBR as translation targets. **Third, the support matrix moves forward.** The spec's "Corona 13–14" should become Corona 14–15 (15 shipped in May 2026), and V-Ray 7.3 is current. **Fourth, the Inspector must audit OpenPBR materials** as the Max 2026+ default, and add rules that catch AI-generator signatures: missing metalness, single global roughness, inverted bump, and non-linear data maps. The forum evidence that exists shows convention mismatches dominate users' pain: DirectX vs OpenGL normals, gloss maps mislabelled as roughness, and normals plugged in without a normal-map node ([Corona forum](https://forum.corona-renderer.com/index.php/topic,11530.0.html), [CGTrader forum](https://cgtrader.com/forum/technical-q-a/please-help-i-have-a-problem-with-exporting-textures-from-substance-painter-to-3ds-max-corona)). Those are exactly the Inspector's existing rule families.

## Conclusion

The competitive picture inverts the original instinct. The feature that looks most futuristic, AI photo-to-material, is the one the renderer vendor now gives away. The features that look mundane are the ones nobody ships: a linter for materials, a roughness curve fitted per engine, an absorption distance computed from a measurement. Every research trend in these notes increases the volume of materials of unknown correctness entering scenes: more AI generators, splat capture, OpenPBR's permitted approximations, agents that build scenes unattended. None of those trends supplies the verifier. That makes the verifier more valuable over time.

The honest form of "ten years ahead" is this: by the time neural bakes and splat capture reach archviz, MatForge should already be the trusted oracle those pipelines call to certify their output. That position is earned through the calibration data and on-box proof gathered in the next twelve months, not through choosing a generator model. The first irreversible step is small and overdue: run the Inspector in a real 3ds Max session.
