# Hyper-realistic PBR material plugin — Plan v3 (audited)

**Provenance.** Plan v2 was audited by 8 independent reviewers (89 raw findings → 36 merged). Every finding was then attacked by 3 adversarial verifiers (findings 31–36 by 2 time-boxed ones) with majority rule: **35 confirmed, 1 refuted**. v3 applies the 35 confirmed corrections using the verified replacement text. Method and limits are in the appendix.

**How to read confidence tags.** [Certain] = verified against primary sources, 3/3 votes. [Likely] = 2/3 or 2/2 votes, or strong inference. [Guessing] = estimate; calibrate in Phase 0/2.

**Still unknown, and still assumed:** host app, audience, team, and what "plugin for anything" means (any surface? any host? an API/MCP service?). §0 makes explicit defaults and §8 Phase 0 forces the decision.

## 1. Define "hyper-realistic" so it is testable

**Colour pipeline — applies to every metric and to the blind test.** [Certain]
- Working space: scene-linear Rec.709, D65 (Blender `Linear Rec.709`, the OCIO `scene_linear` role).
- Reference photo: Bayer RAW → per-CFA black level, white level, saturation mask → lens-shading GainMap → demosaic → camera RGB → XYZ(D50) via DNG ColorMatrix/ForwardMatrix with AsShotNeutral taken from the checker greys → 3×3 (or 2nd-order root-polynomial, Finlayson 2015) colour correction fitted on the 24 ColorChecker patches → Bradford D50→D65. No tone curve, no auto-brightening, no sharpening, no noise reduction.
- Render: Cycles → float OpenEXR in Linear Rec.709 (view transforms never leak into EXR; Film Exposure 1.0).
- Exposure match: one gain fitted by least squares over the six neutral patches (never the white patch alone — Y ≈ 0.91, the first to clip).
- ΔE2000: linear → XYZ (D65) → CIELAB (D65 white) → CIEDE2000 for both images.
- LPIPS, SSIM, blind test: one fixed, named display transform applied identically to both images — Blender "Standard" (piece-wise sRGB) or "Khronos PBR Neutral" (Blender ≥ 4.2) — quantised to 8-bit. Never AgX, never Filmic, never camera JPEGs, and the transform is named in every report.

**Two references.** [Certain]
- (a) White furnace: World = constant (1,1,1), strength 1, no lamps. An energy-conserving material with albedo 1.0 renders as scene-linear 1.0 and vanishes against the background; base-colour swatches read back their stored linear albedo. Any deviation is energy gain or loss.
- (b) Photo comparison under *measured* illumination: either a daylight light booth — D65 (ISO 3668), or D50 (ISO 3664: 2000 lx, CRI ≥ 90) with Bradford adaptation to D65 — rebuilt in Blender as an area emitter of the measured size and position with a neutral matte surround; or an on-site light probe (bracketed mirror-ball or 360° HDRI, Debevec 1997/1998) used as the World. Both sides are normalised so the ColorChecker white patch (Munsell N 9.5, Y ≈ 0.90) reads the same scene-linear value. Focal length, sensor size, pose and f-number are reproduced in the Cycles camera; ISO and shutter enter only through the normalisation. The §4B cross-polarised flash frames are *capture* data, not the reference photo.

**Acceptance criteria.**
1. **Colour, two steps.** [Certain on the structure; Likely on the numbers] (a) Camera chain: mean ΔE2000 ≤ 2.0 and max ≤ 5.0 over the 24 patches (flag 5–6, reject > 6; expected 1.5–3 with a 3×3 matrix, 1–2 with a root-polynomial; mean ≤ 1.0 is a stretch target because charts themselves differ by 0.2–2 ΔE per patch). (b) Material: exported base colour averaged over ≥ 3–5 flat spots vs 45°/0° spectrophotometer or colorimeter readings of the same spots: ΔE2000 ≤ 2.0 (≤ 3.0 for saturated samples). Without an instrument, a second independent capture tests repeatability only, not accuracy.
2. **Pixel metrics only where pixels correspond.** [Certain] LPIPS (`net='alex'`, version 0.1, inputs in [−1, 1]) and SSIM (Wang 2009 reference: luminance, 11×11 Gaussian σ = 1.5, K = [0.01, 0.03], L = 255, automatic downsampling) are computed only on the **capture closure test**: re-render the captured region with the calibrated camera (intrinsics + distortion from ≥ 10 chessboard views, RMS reprojection ≤ 0.5 px) and the calibrated flash positions; undistort and homography-register the RAW photo onto the render's texel grid (residual ≤ 0.5 px); PSF/noise-match or downsample both 4×; score 512-px tiles after the display transform. Procedural, tiled and AI materials never align with a photo and are scored with texture statistics: VGG-19 Gram-matrix distance (Aittala, Aila & Lehtinen 2016) and SIFID, against Phase 0 photos of the same material class.
3. **Thresholds are calibrated, not asserted.** [Certain] Two independent photo sets per Phase 0 sample: same-sample distances define the noise floor, other-sample distances define separation. Pass threshold = 95th percentile of the same-sample distribution for LPIPS, SSIM, Gram and SIFID, confirmed on ≥ 5 held-out samples before freezing. LPIPS ≤ 0.15 and SSIM ≥ 0.85 are placeholders until then. [Guessing]
4. **Blind 2AFC.** [Certain on the design; Likely on the 0.40 bar] Render and matched photo side by side, left/right and order randomised, both through the same crop/resolution/noise/compression pipeline; colorimeter-calibrated sRGB display (D65, 80–160 cd/m², dim neutral surround), ≥ ~60 px per degree, unlimited viewing time; question "which one is the photograph?". p = fraction of trials in which the render is chosen; H0: p ≤ 0.40. A material class passes when the one-sided 95 % Wilson lower bound on p is ≥ 0.40 (p̂ = 0.50 gives 0.419 at n = 100, 0.433 at 150, 0.442 at 200). Budget ≥ 150 trials per class (200 preferred) pooled over ≥ 15–20 naive viewers; confirmatory analysis is a binomial GLMM with viewer and sample random intercepts (10 pairs per viewer at ICC 0.05 → design effect ≈ 1.45). Professional artists are a separate cohort against a bar fixed in Phase 0.
5. **Validator (§6): zero errors.**
6. **Calibration ≠ test.** [Certain] The 20 Phase 0 captures calibrate thresholds and score weights and are then excluded from every gate; all gates run on new, held-out samples.

## 2. Standards and formats

- **Shading model.** [Certain] OpenPBR Surface v1.1.x via MaterialX (nodedef `ND_open_pbr_surface_surfaceshader`, MaterialX ≥ 1.39.0; pin the nodedef version authored against and record it in the asset). The canonical graph uses OpenPBR parameter names and emits the metadata OpenPBR recommends: spec version, working colour space, scene-length → metres factor, texture → parameter association. Length-valued inputs (thickness, height scale, attenuation distance) are MaterialX inputs with `unittype="distance" unit="millimeter"`; `thin_film_thickness` stays in µm. An EXR-header "mm per unit" is internal only — exporters never rely on it. OpenPBR + USD is table stakes, not a differentiator (Sampler 6.0 defaults to OpenPBR and exports USD).
- **BRDF.** [Certain] GGX with multiple-scattering energy compensation (Kulla & Conty 2017 / Turquin 2019; OpenPBR: "should ideally"). Dielectrics: the exact angle-dependent Fresnel from `specular_ior` (default 1.5) — never Schlick in the canonical model (Schlick mishandles total internal reflection in transmissive materials); Schlick/F0 appears only in glTF export. Metals: OpenPBR F82-tint (Kutz, Hašan & Edmondson 2021): F0 = base_weight · base_color, edge tint via `specular_color` ≤ 1. Complex IOR is not a parameter in OpenPBR, standard_surface, glTF, Blender Principled or Unreal Substrate, so the §4D n,k tables are projected **at build time**: conductor Fresnel per wavelength at 0° and 82°, integrated to linear RGB (CIE 1931 2°, D65) — never n,k averaged to RGB first (Hoffman 2019) — into F0 + F82 (OpenPBR, Principled "Specular Tint"), F0 only (glTF), F0/F90 (Substrate), optionally RGB n,k to MaterialX `conductor_bsdf` or Blender's Metallic BSDF "Physical Conductor".
- **Colour-space declaration.** [Certain] Never carried by PNG chunks — no target reads them: glTF requires embedded colour-space information to be ignored; Blender decodes every PNG as its default byte space; MaterialX and USD read only their own declarations. Each exporter writes an explicit per-slot declaration: glTF — encoding implied by slot (sRGB: baseColor, emissive, specularColor, sheenColor; linear: everything else), PNGs written without cHRM/gAMA/iCCP/sBIT/sRGB/cICP chunks; MaterialX — root `colorspace="lin_rec709_scene"`, colour `<image>` inputs `srgb_rec709_scene` (ASWF Color Interop IDs, MaterialX ≥ 1.39.4; legacy-name switch for ≤ 1.39.3); USD — `inputs:sourceColorSpace` "sRGB" (colour) or "raw" (every data map, normals included), plus `UsdColorSpaceAPI` `lin_rec709_scene` on USD ≥ 25.02; Blender — `colorspace_settings.name` 'sRGB' / 'Linear Rec.709' / 'Non-Color'. An undeclared OpenPBR document is assumed ACEScg (AP1 primaries) — wrong primaries, and a ΔE error the metric would charge to the material.
- **Files (master set).** [Certain] Colour maps 8/16-bit PNG, sRGB transfer, Rec.709 primaries. Data maps 16-bit PNG or EXR, linear, never colour-managed. Height EXR float with mm scale as a MaterialX unit attribute. Normal maps tangent-space, MikkTSpace, +Y (OpenGL), linear; the exporter re-derives the normal from height in the target's convention rather than trusting a flag.
- **Export targets** — every target is a derived pack; the exporter logs a per-target drop list. [Certain]

- **Mip policy.** [Certain] Renormalise normals per mip and fold the discarded normal variance into roughness (r² ← min(1, r² + 2σ²); Toksvig 2005, LEAN 2010) so near-field normal detail degrades into far-field roughness instead of vanishing.
- **Projection.** UDIM for UV'd assets; triplanar for un-UV'd meshes; texel density recorded in metadata.
- **Texel density.** 2048 px/m default, 4096 hero, never below 1024/m. [Likely — not contested by the audit]
- **Version contract.** [Certain] In-process add-on code imports only Blender's bundled `MaterialX` and `pxr`; never pip-install PyMaterialX or usd-core into Blender's Python. ## 4. Four creation pipelines

### A. Procedural engine (always available) [Likely]
- Node graph evaluated per 2048² tile (§7): macro shape → meso grain → micro scratches; wear driven by curvature + AO + gravity-dirt masks; every channel derived from one structure signal. The micro-normal band is the highest octaves of the same structure height, made non-repeating by the Heitz–Neyret blend, with its variance carried into roughness by the mip policy — no separately tiled "8–16×" layer.
- Tiling: Heitz–Neyret histogram-preserving blending; Wang tiles for large surfaces; §6 wrap-seam and repetition checks on every output.
- Best for wood, concrete, plaster, weaves, machined metal, paint; weak for organic growth and complex staining. Procedural materials replicate no physical sample, so they are scored REFERENCE-FREE (§6).

### B. Photo → PBR capture — the accuracy source. Tripod, fixed view, moving polarised light. Not handheld. [Certain unless tagged]
- **Rig.** Phone or camera locked on a tripod looking straight down at a flat sample. In frame: ColorChecker Classic, scale marker, chrome ball (light-direction recovery, metal-leak reference), glossy black dielectric sphere (polariser null check). One external continuous LED (a second phone's torch suffices) fitted with linear-polariser film, moved to 8 azimuths in 45° steps (minimum 6) at 45–60° elevation and a recorded distance (≥ 3× the sample diagonal; the far-field model needs ≥ 10×, otherwise model the near-field LED). The lens analyser stays fixed; at every light position the light's polariser is re-rotated until the black sphere's highlight nulls in live view (a chrome ball never nulls fully: oblique metallic reflection is elliptical). Per position: one crossed frame and one parallel frame at identical power and exposure. Plus **one co-located frame from the capturing phone's own flash** — the only frame that samples the specular peak. Crossed frames lose ≥ 2 stops: tripod exposures of 1/15–1 s at base ISO. The phone's flash cannot be the moving light; it is fixed to the lens.
- **Capture format: Bayer RAW only.** JPEG/HEIC (multi-frame fusion, local tone mapping, auto WB, NR, sharpening) and Apple ProRAW (demosaiced, fused, ProfileGainTableMap) are rejected at ingest. iOS 14.3+: AVCapturePhotoOutput with a Bayer RAW pixel format (`isBayerRAWPixelFormat`), `isAppleProRAWEnabled = false`, `photoQualityPrioritization = .speed`, still-image stabilisation off, exposure/ISO/focus/WB locked; 48 MP iPhones deliver Bayer RAW binned to ~12 MP, so plan texel density on that. Android: Camera2 with RAW + MANUAL_SENSOR capabilities (RAW is guaranteed only at hardware level 3), RAW_SENSOR stream, AE/AF/AWB off with fixed exposure, sensitivity and focus distance, lens-shading map on, DngCreator output. Every frame of a sample uses identical settings; white balance is never applied in camera.
- **Ingest / linearise** (`matcore/capture/ingest`): reject anything that is not a Bayer DNG; per-CFA black level; white level; saturation mask; lens-shading GainMap; demosaic; camera RGB → XYZ(D50) via ColorMatrix/ForwardMatrix with AsShotNeutral from the checker greys; checker colour correction on linear data (3×3, or Finlayson 2015 root-polynomial); ΔE2000 reported on held-out patches.
- **Polarisation split.** Per pixel and channel: I_cross = m·D/2, I_par = m·(D/2 + S) — depolarised diffuse splits equally between analyser states, single-bounce specular keeps the source polarisation, m is one global scale. Fix m from the checker neutrals in the cross frame: m = 2·I_cross(neutral)/R_known (published 45/0 reflectance). Then albedo D = 2·I_cross/m and specular S = (I_par − I_cross)/m. Never use I_par + I_cross or an unpolarised frame as albedo (that is D + S, highlight baked in), and never double-scale the cross frame. With an analyser-less frame instead, replace the 2 by r = I_unpol/I_cross measured on a matte neutral (≈ 2.3–2.6 for real film). Clamp S ≥ 0; treat the split as approximate for rough or multi-bounce specular. Metals: the crossed frame does not reach zero (elliptical oblique reflection; depolarising rough metal) — measure the residual on the chrome ball, treat it as leak, and set metal base colour from measured F0 with the diffuse term zero wherever metallic = 1.
- **Normals.** Calibrated **near-light** photometric stereo (Quéau et al., JMIV 2018): each light modelled as a point source with calibrated 3-D position and intensity (white checkerboard plane in ≥ 3 poses, or two mirror spheres) and inverse-square × cosine fall-off; every position flat-fielded with a white planar reference at the sample plane. Input = the cross-polarised (diffuse) stack; ≥ 3 non-coplanar lights after per-pixel shadow/saturation rejection (target 6–8 so ≥ 3 survive). The textbook distant-light solve is wrong here: a light 0.4 m above and beside a 0.3 m sample spans 58°–36° incidence and ~3× irradiance across it. Translucent materials (skin, wax, marble, leaf): subsurface scattering blurs diffuse normals — use specular normals from the I_par − I_cross stack where the specular field is dense (Ma 2007; Chen, Goesele & Seidel 2006), diffuse normals elsewhere.
- **Height.** Neumann-boundary Poisson solve (DCT; Simchony, Chellappa & Shao 1990) or Harker–O'Leary regularised least squares — never plain Frankot–Chellappa on a non-tileable field (its periodic-boundary assumption biases the solution); FC is fine once the normal map is tileable. Integration amplifies low-frequency normal error and cannot recover mean height: on known-flat samples fit and remove a ≤ 2nd-order trend and record it. Valid for relief ≤ ~5 mm over 300 mm. Anything with macro shape (bark curvature, leather folds, cobbles) gets a macro-geometry path — a few photogrammetry frames or a depth scan — fused as the low-frequency band (Nehab et al. 2005), mm-scaled from the marker.
- **Roughness.** One GGX α per material (or per texture class) fitted from the co-located flash frame: with light and camera coincident the half-angle equals the view angle, so the highlight's radial fall-off sweeps θh from 0° at frame centre to ≈ 25–40° at the edges and traces the lobe directly, under a stationarity prior (Aittala, Weyrich & Lehtinen 2015). The 6–8 oblique frames constrain α only for α ≳ 0.3: at 45° elevation they meet the frame centre at θh = 22.5°, where GGX returns 0.4 % / 4.9 % / 16 % of peak for α 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3. Spatial roughness variation is therefore **prior-derived** — texture-statistics regularisation (Aittala 2015/2016) or a multi-image network (Deschaintre 2019; Gao 2019; MaterialGAN) refined by differentiable re-rendering of the captured frames — never from supervised per-pixel labels the rig cannot produce. The validator tags roughness "prior-derived"; the score reports it separately from the measured channels. True per-pixel lobe width needs a non-phone rig: a light dome or arc with direction spacing ≤ 2× the lobe's half-width (α 0.1 → ≈ 7° spacing, 55–120 directions), or spherical-gradient illumination.
- **Tiling.** Patch-based synthesis + histogram-preserving blending; §6 seam and repetition tests on the output.
- Output passes §6 or is rejected; material.json records device, lens, polariser, flash positions, checker serial and calibration hashes.

### C. AI generation and decomposition [Likely unless tagged]
- **Channels.** basecolor, normal, height, roughness, metallic, optional opacity head. **No thickness or translucency channel**: no permissively licensed dataset carries one (MatSynth: basecolor, diffuse, normal, height, roughness, metallic, specular, opacity, displacement, blend_mask) and thickness is per-object geometry a texture-space model cannot know. Thickness is baked in the Blender adapter (Cycles AO node with *Inside*, baked as an Emission pass; or `BVHTree.ray_cast`); translucency colour and mean free path come from §4D scalars. [Certain]
- **Model: adapt, do not train from scratch.** Base: Stable Diffusion 2.x (U-Net fine-tuned; VAE and OpenCLIP text encoder frozen; CreativeML OpenRAIL++-M) or StableMaterials where OpenRAIL terms are acceptable. Recipe (Marigold / Marigold-IID, Apache-2.0 code): encode every target map with the frozen VAE as a 3-channel image — base colour as is, height replicated ×3, roughness + metallic packed into one image; 3 target latents = 12 U-Net output channels; the conditioning photo latent concatenated at input (conv_in 16 channels); new weights initialised by tiling and averaging the pretrained ones; Adam 3e-5, effective batch 32 (micro-batch 2 × 16 accumulation), 20k–40k iterations on 512-px crops. Text-to-material first (prompts from MatSynth name/category/tags, VLM-captioned — only 572 MatSynth materials carry descriptions), then photo decomposition RGB↔X-style by feeding the photo latent. No ControlNet or IP-Adapter needed. Training a new multi-channel VAE (ControlMat route, ≈ 5,300 A100-hours) is out of scope. [Certain on the compute]
- **Tiling and resolution.** Circular padding on all convolutions in training and sampling + feature/noise rolling (StableMaterials / ControlMat); 2–4K via patched diffusion with overlapping-tile latent fusion followed by a tile-aware SDEdit refiner or upscaler (StableMaterials' refiner is unreleased — build it); §6 seam test on the final rolled output. Without this machinery every output fails the seam rule and §2 density.
- **Compute and shipping.** ≈ 2.5 days per 18k iterations on one RTX 4090 (Marigold); budget 3–7 GPU-days per run, 3–5 runs (≤ ~30 GPU-days of the 10-week phase). LCM-distil the final U-Net to 4 steps. End-user inference: NVIDIA GPU ≥ 8 GB VRAM in fp16, 12 GB recommended; LCM lowers latency, not VRAM.
- **Data and licences.** [Certain as of the audit date] Corpus: MatSynth (Vecchio & Deschaintre, CVPR 2024) — 4,069 tileable 4K materials from ambientCG, CGBookCase, Poly Haven, ShareTextures, TextureCan (CC0) plus a small Julio Sillet set (CC-BY), with a per-material `license` field and a fixed 3,980 / 89 train/test split. That is essentially the whole public CC0 corpus (StableMaterials trained on 6,198 and still needed 4,000 synthetic texture–text pairs), so: 512-px crops, scale/rotation/flip augmentation applied identically to every map (re-encode normal vectors), hue/exposure jitter on base colour only, a semi-supervised branch on unannotated RGB textures. Hold out MatSynth's 89 test materials and **every pipeline-B capture** (the only photo-paired data); none enter training. Licence rule — replaces "CC0 or nothing": train only on assets whose licence grants ML use: CC0 (ambientCG; Poly Haven lists AI/ML training as an encouraged use), MatSynth (CC0 + attributed CC-BY), own captures; Poliigon only under its separate written AI/ML licence; Fab listings (Megascans included) only if not tagged NoAI at transaction time (Fab EULA §16(l)); legacy Megascans under the Epic Content License off-limits until its generative-AI clause has been read in the version in force. Per-asset training manifest: source, licence, URL, acquisition date, NoAI tag.
- **Lineage.** [Certain] Generation (text/photo → joint stack): MatFuse (CVPR 2024; diffuse/normal/roughness/specular only) → ControlMat / MatGen (Adobe, ACM TOG 2024; joint basecolor/normal/height/roughness/metallic/opacity, ControlNet photo conditioning, noise rolling, patched diffusion) → StableMaterials (public data, 4-step LCM, weights on Hugging Face) → MaterialPicker (Adobe, SIGGRAPH 2025). Multi-channel adaptation recipes: RGB↔X (SD 2.1, frozen VAE; weights are Adobe Research License, non-commercial — borrow the recipe, not the weights), Collaborative Control (ECCV 2024), Marigold. Decomposition: Deschaintre 2018 → SurfaceNet (ICCV 2021) → ControlMat's ControlNet branch. MaterialGAN is a StyleGAN2 prior for 3–7-flash inverse rendering — relevant only if pipeline B adds an optimisation stage; Material Palette is a UX reference, not an architecture. Shipping competitors the generator is measured against: Adobe Image to Material (ControlMat-based), Unity 6 AI Material Generator, NVIDIA RTX Remix AI Texture Tools — all infer roughness without measuring it.
- **Physics projection** — mandatory post-pass, then the validator. (i) Albedo clamp with bounds interpolated by metalness m: lower = lerp(dielectric_min, F0_min, m), upper = lerp(dielectric_max, 1, m). (ii) Metalness: outside the transition/contamination mask a soft bimodal prior (smoothstep sharpening around 0.5); the mask comes from an independent source (prompt tag, user layer, separate channel) — never from thresholding the metalness map itself. (iii) Normal/height **fusion, not derivation**: integrate the generated normal (FC is valid here — the output is tileable), gain-fit to the generated height's low band, take the low band (crossover ≈ 1/16 tile, tuned on Phase 0 captures) from height and the higher bands from the normal, rebuild the normal so both agree at every frequency. (iv) Roughness kept as a prior — report the §6 dependence metric; never re-correlate to height values. Log per-map PSNR and the fraction of texels moved as a model-quality metric.
- **Product feature:** import → validate → correct → re-export for third-party and AI materials (Sampler, Unity, Remix output), with their roughness treated as a prior.

### D. Measured reference library [Certain]
- **Metals.** Complex n,k from refractiveindex.info (database CC0 1.0; store page id and paper per entry). Visible-range data exist for ~27 bare metals plus alloy pages (stainless, Cu–Zn brass, Au–Ag, Ni–Fe, AuAl2) and TiN — budget **~30 metals, not ~40**. Pinned datasets: Rakić 1998 Brendel–Bormann (exactly Ag, Al, Au, Be, Cr, Cu, Ni, Pd, Pt, Ti, W), Johnson & Christy 1972 (Ag, Au, Cu) and 1974 (Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Pd), Querry 1985/87, McPeak 2015, Babar & Weaver 2015, Palm 2018 (Ti film), Weaver 1973–77. Exclude REELS-derived Werner 2009 for visible F0 (unphysical Ti 0.94, Pt 0.93, W 0.84). Palik's Handbook is not on the site and not CC0. Hf, Re, Ru (EUV-only) and Sn (IR-only) have no visible data. Projected to F0 + F82 at build time (§2).
- **Dielectrics.** ~60 IORs: glass by family (fused silica 1.458; borosilicate ≈ 1.47; N-BK7 1.517; soda-lime 1.514–1.523; lead crystal ≥ 1.545; F2 1.620; SF10 1.728; SF11 1.785; N-SF66 1.923), ice 1.310, water 1.333, sapphire 1.76, cubic zirconia 2.159, diamond 2.417, moissanite, rutile; glass absorption from Rubin 1985 and Vogt 2016 (§3).
- **Scattering.** Jensen et al. 2001 (skin1, skin2, marble, milk, cream, ketchup), Narasimhan et al. 2006 (liquids). No row without a measured source; no row, no "measured" badge.
- **Cycles SSS mapping** (Subsurface Radius × Scale in scene metres; the exporter branches on version and method): Blender ≥ 5.2 "Random Walk" uses the product directly as 1/σt → Skin1 Radius (1.0, 0.735, 0.518), Scale 0.0013 m. Blender 4.0–5.1 "Random Walk" (incl. 4.5 LTS) and ≥ 5.2 "Random Walk (Legacy)" multiply by 1/(4π) → enter 4π × MFP: same Radius, Scale 0.0163 m. "Random Walk (Skin)" (≥ 4.0) takes the diffusion length 1/σtr → Radius (1.0, 0.372, 0.186), Scale 0.00367 m, Base Color = Jensen's dipole Rd (0.44, 0.23, 0.13) linear, Subsurface IOR 1.3. Blender's default Radius (1.0, 0.2, 0.1) is an artistic default. The validator's SSS check compares in the target renderer's convention.
- Every preset in A and C snaps to these values. UI badges: **measured** / **preset** / **prior-derived** / **artistic**.

## 5. Per-channel realism techniques

- **Colour.** [Certain] Working space Linear Rec.709 (D65). Black/white-level linearisation, lens shading and the checker-derived white point and matrix are applied to Bayer RAW before anything else; never ingest JPEG/HEIC/ProRAW. Add low-frequency hue drift — real objects are never one hue.
- **Roughness.** [Likely] Three-layer composite: material base → wear/polish (curvature-driven, lowers roughness) → contamination (fingerprints, dust; raises roughness). Histogram non-Gaussian. The validator measures dependence on curvature/slope/cavity features, not on height value.
- **Normal / height.** [Likely] In A and B generate height first and derive the normal; in C fuse (§4C). The exported normal equals the height gradient at low/mid frequency. The micro band is the highest octaves of the same structure height with a texel-footprint cutoff, not a fixed 8–16× layer; its variance folds into roughness per mip.
- **Transparency.** [Certain] Glass: transmission 1.0; roughness 0.0–0.05 (≥ 0.005 wherever a sampled, non-delta lobe is wanted — fingerprints, haze); surface fingerprints; IOR by glass family (§4D); a measured absorption colour at a stated distance (§3); thin film only where measured; dispersion optional (Abbe number where the target supports it). Opacity maps only for leaves, mesh, lace.
- **Translucency.** [Certain] σa/σs′ from §4D per material; thickness baked per object; random-walk SSS; a backlight test render for every translucent material.
- **Energy conservation.** [Certain] A property of the layered BSDF, not of textures — OpenPBR's glossy-diffuse base conserves energy for any base colour ≤ 1, so the per-pixel "diffuse + specular ≤ 1 after Fresnel" rule is dropped (trivially true at normal incidence, false at grazing). Replace with (i) map checks — dielectric luminance range, metal F0 inside the §4D envelope (≈ 0.45–0.99 linear) — and (ii) a white-furnace test of each target renderer's BSDF (roughness sweeps of an F0 = 1 metal and a white dielectric must vanish against the background; tolerance from Phase 0). Print the renderer and its compensation setting beside every score (Cycles: "Multiscatter GGX"); the score certifies only that path. The glTF exporter warns about the uncompensated Appendix-B BRDF.

## 6. Validator (automated, runs on every export)

**Errors — block export.** [Likely unless tagged]
- Dielectric base-colour luminance < 30 sRGB on a non-metal pixel (strict mode: < 50).
- Raw-metal F0 off the n,k-derived value by > 0.05 linear on any channel (pipeline C output; a warning for captures).
- Metal non-bimodal: > 5 % of texels in (0.05, 0.95) after excluding a 2-px dilated metal/dielectric boundary band and declared contamination masks.
- Normal convention/consistency failure: corr(n.x, −∂h/∂x) or corr(n.y, −∂h/∂y) < 0.9, or n.z ≤ 0 anywhere.
- Roughness spatially constant (fewer than two distinct code values).
- Baked AO detected (dark-pixel correlation with cavity) — kept from v2; the finding against it was refuted.
- Colour-space declaration missing or wrong in an exported document (glTF slot encoding, MaterialX `colorspace`, USD `sourceColorSpace`, Blender `colorspace_settings`), or colour-space chunks present in glTF PNGs — checked on the documents, never by reading PNG chunks. [Certain]
- Wrap seam: the signed difference across each wrap boundary (I[:,0] − I[:,−1]; I[0,:] − I[−1,:]), averaged along the seam, differs from the same statistic at interior columns/rows by > 4σ (initial; calibrate on Phase 0); the same test on slopes (4–16 px windows at 3 scales) and on the derived normal. Autocorrelation is **not** a seam test — any tiled image is periodic by construction. [Certain]
- Missing physical scale / unit attributes.

**Warnings.**
- Luminance 30–50 sRGB; > 240 sRGB unless whitelisted (fresh snow, PTFE/Spectralon).
- Roughness clipping: > 0.5 % of texels at exactly 0.0 or 1.0 outside a declared mirror/polish mask; roughness variance below threshold.
- Maps uncorrelated: bias-corrected dependence (NMI at 32–64 bins minus the mean of ≥ 3 shuffle baselines, or distance correlation on ≥ 2,000 texels) between roughness and height-*derived* features (curvature, slope, cavity at σ = 1, 4, 16 px) below the 10th percentile of the Phase 0 corpus. Never raw MI of roughness against height values.
- Visible repetition: the single tile's normalised circular autocorrelation has an isolated local maximum ≥ 0.6 beyond the central lobe (> 16 px), ignoring directional-grain ridges.
- IOR tiers (§3); SSS coefficients outside the measured table (compared in the target renderer's convention); texel density below target.
- Roughness tagged "prior-derived" (phone pipeline, single-image AI).
- Per-target packing/compression checks; mip-chain roughness monotonicity (r² non-decreasing per mip).

**Metrics and score.** [Certain on the structure; Guessing on weights]
- ΔE2000 on checker patches (camera chain) and against instrument readings (material); LPIPS (alex, v0.1) and Wang-2009 SSIM only on the registered capture closure test; VGG Gram and SIFID texture distances for every pipeline; 2AFC where run.
- **Realism score (0–100) = 100 × Σ wᵢ·sᵢ**, documented, every component shown beside the scalar with versions ("realism 74 · validator 1.2.0 · score 1.0.3 · tables 2025.11"). **MEASURED** mode (a pixel-registered reference exists): camera-chain ΔE, material ΔE, LPIPS/SSIM on the registered pair (record homography and residual; switch to ST-LPIPS when residual > 1 px), validator pass rate. **REFERENCE-FREE** mode (procedural, AI): per-class texture statistics against ≥ 50 CC0 photos per material class — albedo-histogram distance, roughness skew/excess kurtosis, 1/f^β power-spectrum slope of height and albedo, roughness–curvature dependence — plus KID (unbiased MMD, degree-3 polynomial kernel; not FID, which is biased at small n) between ≥ 1,000 rendered 256-px patches and ≥ 1,000 photo patches of that class, with n and feature extractor reported. Weights fitted on the 20 Phase 0 captures and frozen; the score may gate anything only after it reaches Spearman ρ ≥ 0.6 with the 2AFC pick rate over ≥ 30 materials including ≥ 10 held-out captures.
- Validator precision/recall measured in Phase 1 on ≥ 50 known-good scans (own captures + ambientCG/Poly Haven) and ≥ 50 deliberately broken goldens: false-positive rate < 5 %, recall 100 %.

**Fixed in Phase 1.** [Certain]
- **IPC boundary.** Add-on and sidecar exchange only files (PNG/EXR/MaterialX on disk) and serialised messages (JSON or protobuf over a local socket or stdin/stdout) — never Blender data structures, never shared memory (the GPL FAQ treats shared complex structures as equivalent to linking). The sidecar ships as its own installer, not inside the add-on zip. Budget the latency of moving 4K texture sets across the boundary.
- **Evaluation model.** Graphs evaluate per 2048² tile with filter halos under an explicit VRAM budget: an 8K RGBA float32 map is 1 GiB and the §3 stack is 8–12 maps before intermediates. Interactive preview is capped at 2K; full-resolution bakes are background jobs. GPU kernels are float32; float64 height integration runs on the CPU. Procedural and capture kernels are written against this tile ABI in their final language from day one (Slang or wgpu for portability; Triton/CUDA only where Linux/NVIDIA-only is acceptable). "Rust/wgpu later" is a full kernel rewrite, not a hot-loop swap — wgpu's default limits (256 MiB buffers, 128 MiB storage bindings, 8192-px textures) force tiling there too. PyTorch is kept for `ml/` only.
- **Threading.** Blender's Python is not thread-safe (its docs: Python threads "cause Blender to crash in hard to diagnose ways"); all long-running work lives in the sidecar, and the add-on only polls.
- **Provenance.** Every material version ships a signed, schema-versioned `material.json`: source type (captured / procedural / AI / mixed); capture metadata (device, lens, polariser, flash positions, checker serial, scale marker) with SHA-256 of calibration files; registration homography + residual; pipeline/validator/score/reference-table versions (semver + git hash); render settings (Cycles version, illuminant + hash, exposure, samples, OCIO config); every metric with the SHA-256 of its reference image; SHA-256 of every map; a detached signature over the canonicalised JSON (C2PA-style signed claim). Key fields are duplicated as MaterialX custom attributes (`<attributedef>`) and in glTF `extras`. A validator or score upgrade re-scores the whole library and rewrites sidecars.
- **Round-trip test.** 20 golden `.mtlx` → `mtlx_to_nodes` → Cycles, and Blender nodes → `nodes_to_mtlx` → validator → `mtlx_to_nodes` → Cycles; fixed seed and sample count, denoiser off, on every supported Blender build; pass when per-channel |Δ| in 8-bit sRGB has mean ≤ 1/255 and 99th percentile ≤ 4/255 with lost-parameter channels masked. glTF round-trip runs through Blender's bundled `io_scene_gltf2`, scoped to the extensions it implements.

## 9. Risks

- **GPL exposure.** Blender's reading (anything bundled with an add-on that integrates through the API should be GPL) is stricter than the FSF FAQ and the derivative-work question is contested; option (b) is reviewed by counsel before Phase 1 code exists. [Certain on the positions]
- **Distribution.** Option (b) cannot list on extensions.blender.org (no payment/key gating, no external functional components, no obfuscated code, ~200 MB cap); it sells via Superhive or an own store, and any extensions.blender.org listing must be a complete, free GPL add-on. [Certain]
- **Dependency install failure** (offline/proxy, disk, driver or CUDA mismatch, unsupported GPU). Engine degrades to CPU-only; the add-on reports the support-matrix state; never a half-installed torch. [Certain]
- **Demand.** Two funded vendors already retreated: Unity bought Artomatix (2020), cut ArtEngine to $19/month (2021), then discontinued it; Adobe removed Sampler's 3D Capture (5.1, Aug 2025) and its Firefly generative panel (gone in 6.0). Mitigation: WTP test at week 10; capture before procedural so the first external blind test (~week 17) uses captured materials; kill criterion in §8. [Certain on the history; Likely on the mitigation]
- **Training-data and model-weight licensing.** Manifest rule (§4C). Base-checkpoint terms recorded: Apache-2.0/MIT bases carry no pass-through restrictions; CreativeML OpenRAIL-M/++ permit commercial use but require passing their use restrictions on; Stability Community License is free only below US$1M revenue and covers fine-tunes; FLUX.1 [dev]-family weights are non-commercial and "Derivative" includes fine-tunes. Do not ship fine-tunes of MatFuse or StableMaterials weights without clearance (both trained partly on the Deschaintre 2018 INRIA set, reported CC BY-NC-ND; StableMaterials' card has no licence text); MaterialGAN's repository has no licence file. Ship an attribution file. Decide commercial vs not **before** Phase 5 — it selects the admissible base checkpoints. [Certain]
- **Colour management.** One conversion module — sRGB ↔ linear, Linear Rec.709 ↔ XYZ (D65), XYZ ↔ CIELAB, Bradford D50 → D65, Khronos PBR Neutral — pinned to one OCIO config version, each conversion unit-tested against published reference values. [Certain]
- **Phone computational photography** breaks linearity and frame-to-frame repeatability. Bayer RAW only, locked settings, lens-shading map, empirical linearity test in Phase 0; no vendor documents exposure proportionality, so it is measured, not assumed. [Certain]
- **Tiling artefacts.** Wrap-boundary z-score (values, slopes, normals) plus the repetition warning from Phase 1; thresholds from Phase 0 captures. [Certain]
- **AI output that looks right but is physically wrong.** Projection is mandatory, never a toggle; Phase 5 measures the model *before* projection so the clamp cannot hide a bad generator. [Likely]
- **Host/audience mismatch.** A Phase 0 exit criterion, not an assumption. [Certain]
- **Scope creep into a node editor.** Engine headless; reuse Blender's node UI. [Likely]

## 10. Hard rules

Never bake lighting into albedo. Never eyeball IOR or scattering — every value comes from a §4D row with a source, or carries an artistic / preset / prior-derived badge. Never import torch, or anything GPU-heavy, into Blender's Python. Never read PNG chunks for colour space — declare it per target. Never treat single-image or 6–8-flash roughness as measured. Never generate maps independently — one structure signal, and the normal must equal the height gradient. Never export without the validator. Never ship a material without a reference render, a signed material.json and the validator/score versions printed beside its score. Never gate a phase on a score that has not been validated against the blind test.

## Appendix — how v3 was produced, and its limits

- 8 independent auditors (physics/numbers, standards/formats, capture feasibility, ML/data, architecture/licensing, validation methodology, market, plan realism) produced 89 raw findings, merged to 36. Each was verified by 3 adversarial agents — one trying to refute the problem, one judging materiality, one checking the proposed correction (findings 31–36 by 2 time-boxed agents) — with majority rule. 35 confirmed, 1 refuted (#17, the baked-AO rule, kept as in v2). Verifiers wrote and ran their own numeric checks (Mueller calculus, GGX lobe ratios, Wilson intervals, seam/autocorrelation simulations, n,k → F0 integration, MI estimator bias) where the claim allowed it.
- Some primary hosts were unreachable from the sandbox (arxiv.org, docs.blender.org, helpx.adobe.com, huggingface.co, several vendor sites); verifiers marked those claims as cited from prior knowledge, and the plan tags them [Likely] rather than [Certain]. Most exposed: Adobe product dates (#20), archviz survey percentages (#36), ControlMat training-set sizes (#24, #31).
- Every threshold tagged [Guessing] is a placeholder that Phase 0/2 calibration replaces; the plan's structure does not depend on the current values.
- Phase durations remain estimates. The audit checked their arithmetic and dependencies, not their realism for your specific team.
